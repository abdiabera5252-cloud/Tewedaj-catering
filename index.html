About the kitchen

Every wat starts the same way it would in a family kitchen — spices roasted that morning, onions caramelized low and unhurried, nothing shortcut for the sake of a bigger table.

Tewedaj began as cooking for family gatherings and grew, one referral at a time, into full-service catering for weddings, offices, and holidays. The kitchen still runs on the same rule: injera fermented properly, berbere ground fresh, and every dish tasted before it leaves the pot. Whether it's twelve people around a mesob or three hundred at a hall, that's the standard.

How it's cooked

Four steps, nothing rushed

The timeline behind every order — most of it happens before you ever see a menu.

01

Spice roasted fresh

Berbere and mitmita are roasted and ground the morning of your event — not weeks ahead in bulk.

02

Injera ferments for three days

Teff batter sits and sours properly, which is where the signature tang actually comes from.

03

Simmered low and slow

Wats cook for hours in niter kibbeh, stirred by hand, tasted and adjusted through the afternoon.

04

Served family style

Set out on a mesob and shared by hand — the way the meal is meant to be eaten, at any table size.
